Outside, the first birds were waking up. Inside, Mirai was still asleep, curled up small and warm.

Ever since the day Mirai was born, Mama had called her by one special word.

Mama bent down and whispered, warm against her ear:

"Wake up, Koshakanym." said Mama.

It was Mama's word. Just for Mirai.

Warm water ran from the tap. Splish, splash. Mirai caught it in her hooves, and it wriggled right through.

"It tickles!" said Mirai.

Mama flicked one tiny drop onto her cheek.

"Mama! Now I'm all drippy!" said Mirai.

"A drippy Koshakan." said Mama.

"And a very clean one." said Mama.

The meadow smelled of grass and warm earth. Mirai ran, and ran, and jumped, right into the dandelions.

Puff! Puff! Puff!

"Mama, look! I'm making snow!" said Mirai.

"Summer snow! Run, Koshakanym, run!" said Mama.

It was Mama's word. Just for Mirai.

Faster, faster… whoops! Down went Mirai, bump, into the grass. It was cool and tickly on her back.

Mama sat down beside her.

"Up you get, Koshakanym." said Mama.

But Mirai didn't get up. She laughed and laughed until her tummy wobbled. So Mama laughed too.

"Oh, what a giggly Koshakan you are!" said Mama.

Mirai climbed into Mama's lap. It was soft and warm, like a nest in the grass.

High above them, a slow white cloud drifted by.

"Rest with me, Koshakanym." said Mama.

"Just for a little bit." said Mirai.

Mirai was getting bigger every day. But Mama still called her the very same thing.

It was Mama's word. Just for Mirai.

Mirai watched the cloud for a long, long time. A dandelion seed floated past her nose.

She had never wondered before. But today, she did.

"Mama?" asked Mirai.

"Mm?" asked Mama.

"You always call me Koshakan." said Mirai.

"What does Koshakan mean?" asked Mirai.

Mama held her a little closer.

"Koshakan means a little lamb." said Mama.

"And Koshakanym means my little lamb." said Mama.

"A lamb?" asked Mirai.

"A lamb is small and tender." said Mama.

Its wool is soft as dandelion fluff, and warm as milk at bedtime. A lamb is gentle. And kind. All the way through.

"When you sleep, curled up tight," said Mama.

I see a little lamb. When you run through the grass, I see a little lamb. And when you laugh… I hear one.

"A Koshakan is someone loved very, very much." said Mama.

Someone you always, always take care of.

Mirai didn't say anything. Mama didn't say anything either. Even the dandelions held still.

Mirai laid her ear on Mama's chest and listened.

Thump-thump. Thump-thump. Slow and soft and close.

It was Mama's heart. Just for Mirai.

Somewhere in the grass, the crickets began to sing.

Mirai lifted her head. She looked at her own small hooves. Then up at Mama. She spoke so softly that only Mama could hear.

"Mama… so I'm like a little lamb?" asked Mirai.

"Yes." said Mama.

"My little, most-loved Koshakan." said Mama.

Mirai hugged Mama tight. Tighter. As tight as she possibly could.

"Then I'll always be your Koshakan." said Mirai.

"Always and always." said Mirai.

"Always, Koshakanym." said Mama.

The sun was warm on their backs.

From that day on, Koshakan was never just a word. It felt like a hug.

It was Mama's word. And now it was Mirai's, too.
